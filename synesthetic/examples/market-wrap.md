## The week in brief

The S&P 500 closed Friday at 6,912.48, up 1.6% for the week (+108.73 points), its third weekly gain in four weeks. The Nasdaq Composite rose 2.1%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 0.9%, and the small-cap Russell 2000 slipped 0.4%.

Volatility eased: the VIX ended at 14.2, down from 16.8 the Friday before. The 10-year Treasury yield fell 9 basis points to 4.18%, and the 2-year yield fell 6 basis points to 3.86%. The dollar index lost 0.7%, gold rose 1.2% to $2,684 an ounce, and West Texas crude dropped 3.4% to $68.15 a barrel.

Breadth improved but stayed narrow. 61% of S&P 500 members finished the week higher, yet the ten largest companies accounted for about 58% of the index's gain. See the sector scoreboard on page 2 and Figure 1 for the full breakdown.

## Day by day

- Monday, Nov 9: stocks opened the week quietly, up 0.4%, as traders waited for inflation data. Volume was 12% below the 20-day average.
- Tuesday, Nov 10: the Consumer Price Index, released at 8:30 AM ET, showed prices up 2.7% from a year earlier, below the 2.9% forecast. Core CPI rose 0.2% for the month. The index gained 1.1%, its best day since Sep 18.
- Wednesday, Nov 11: the bond market was closed for Veterans Day while stocks traded. The index was flat, finishing down 0.02%.
- Thursday, Nov 12: the Producer Price Index matched forecasts at 0.1%. Weekly jobless claims came in at 214,000. The index slipped 0.3% after a two-day run.
- Friday, Nov 13: the University of Michigan consumer sentiment reading rose to 71.4 from 68.9. Stocks closed up 0.4%, near the session high, at 4:00 PM ET.

## Earnings

With 94% of S&P 500 companies reporting, third-quarter earnings are tracking growth of 8.9% from a year earlier, above the 6.1% expected at the end of September. 77% of companies beat profit estimates, close to the five-year average of 78%.

Three reports moved the market this week (all three companies are invented for this sample):

1. Northwind Systems reported earnings of $2.41 a share against $2.18 expected, on revenue of $14.6 billion, up 19%. Shares rose 8.3%.
2. Contoso Retail cut its full-year outlook to $5.10 to $5.25 a share from $5.60, citing weaker holiday traffic. Shares fell 11.7%, the worst move in the index.
3. Fabrikam Energy beat estimates but warned that lower oil prices would weigh on the fourth quarter. Shares slipped 2.2%.

The quarter's season ends next week with 14 more S&P 500 companies due, including two large retailers on Nov 18 and Nov 19.

## Rates and the Fed

Softer inflation pushed rate-cut expectations higher. Futures now put a 62% chance on a quarter-point cut at the Federal Reserve meeting on Dec 15 to Dec 16, up from 41% a week ago.

"The market is pricing a soft landing, not a perfect one," said Dana Whitfield, chief strategist at Harbor Point Advisors. "One good inflation report is welcome. It is not a trend yet."

Mortgage rates followed Treasury yields lower: the average 30-year fixed rate fell to 6.12% from 6.31%, according to a weekly lender survey.

## The technical picture

The index is 1.3% below its record close of 7,004.10 from Oct 27. First support sits near 6,780, about the 50-day moving average of 6,742. Resistance is the record itself, then the round number 7,000. Momentum readings turned positive on Tuesday; see Section 4 and Figure 2 for the chart.

## Risks to watch

Warning: one strong week is not a trend. Do not read Tuesday's inflation number as proof that price pressure is over; the next CPI report on Dec 10 matters as much.

- The rally is narrow. If the largest ten stocks stall, the index can fall even while most stocks hold up.
- Oil is falling fast. Cheaper fuel helps consumers, but a drop below $65 a barrel has in the past signaled slowing demand.
- Liquidity thins out over the holidays. Thanksgiving falls on Nov 26, and trading closes early at 1:00 PM ET on Nov 27. Expect bigger moves on lighter volume.
- A government funding deadline on Jan 30, 2027 is required reading for bond investors, not just a headline risk.
